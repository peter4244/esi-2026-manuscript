Rationale. A recently proposed multidimensional diagnostic framework for COPD (MD-COPD) integrates spirometry, respiratory symptoms, and chest computed tomography (CT), resulting in better alignment of COPD diagnosis with disease-related risk than the fixed FEV₁/FVC ratio. However, requiring CT may limit its implementation. One benefit of MD-COPD is the identification of an airflow limitation only (AFL-only) group whose mortality is similar to that of non-obstructed participants.

Objectives. To determine whether the Emphysema Severity Index (ESI), derived from the expiratory portion of the flow-volume curve, can effectively substitute for CT criteria in the MD-COPD classification when CT is unavailable.

Methods. Among 9,240 ever-smokers in COPDGene, we compared MD-COPD with modified classifications in which CT criteria were removed with no replacement (NoCT) or with ESI as a substitute (ESI classification). Risks of mortality, respiratory exacerbations, and longitudinal FEV₁ decline were assessed.

Measurements and Main Results. Among participants with airflow limitation, ESI discriminated visual emphysema and definite airway wall thickening with an AUC of 0.75 for each. The NoCT classification reassigned 833 MD-COPD COPD-major participants to the AFL-only group, compared with only 350 using ESI. Relative to MD-COPD AFL-only, NoCT-defined AFL-only had higher unadjusted all-cause mortality (rate ratio, 1.38; 95% CI, 1.10–1.77) and exacerbation rates (1.42; 95% CI, 1.15–1.79), whereas the ESI-defined AFL-only group did not differ from MD-COPD AFL-only.

Conclusions. When CT is unavailable, removing its criteria from MD-COPD loses clinically relevant information. Incorporating ESI recovers part of this information, particularly among participants with airflow limitation, without requiring a test beyond conventional spirometry.
