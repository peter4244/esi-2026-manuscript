# Abstract — CT-free MD-COPD paper

**Rationale.** A recently proposed multidimensional diagnostic framework for COPD (MD-COPD) provides better alignment of COPD diagnosis with disease-related risk than the fixed FEV₁/FVC ratio. However, the MD-COPD classification requires chest CT data, limiting its applicability in some contexts.

**Objectives.** To determine whether the Emphysema Severity Index (ESI), derived from the expiratory portion of the flow-volume curve, can substitute for CT criteria in the MD-COPD classification.

**Methods.** Among 9,240 ever-smokers in COPDGene, we compared MD-COPD with modified classifications in which CT criteria were removed with no replacement (NoCT) or with ESI as a substitute (ESI classification). Risks of mortality, respiratory exacerbations, and FEV₁ decline were assessed.

**Measurements and Main Results.** The ESI classification resulted in better reconstruction of the MD-COPD framework than the NoCT classification (89% versus 85% agreement, P < 0.005) and provided reasonable discrimination of visual CT criteria for emphysema and airway wall thickening (AUC 0.78 and 0.80, respectively). ESI substantially reduced the misassignment of MD-COPD COPD-major participants to the low-risk airflow limitation only (AFL-only) group: 833 of 3,809 (21.9%) under NoCT against 350 (9.2%) under ESI. Relative to MD-COPD AFL-only, NoCT AFL-only participants had higher unadjusted all-cause mortality (rate ratio 1.38 [95% CI 1.10–1.77]) and exacerbation rates (rate ratio 1.42 [1.15–1.79]), whereas rates in ESI-defined AFL-only were not significantly different (1.14 [0.91–1.44] and 0.96 [0.78–1.21]).

**Conclusions.** When CT is unavailable, incorporating ESI in a modified MD-COPD classification preserves some of the benefits of the MD-COPD classification.
