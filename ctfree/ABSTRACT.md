Rationale. A multidimensional diagnostic framework for COPD (MD-COPD) combining post-bronchodilator spirometry, respiratory symptoms and chest computed tomography (CT) refines the COPD diagnostic criteria to be better aligned with COPD-related disease risks than the traditional fixed FEV₁/FVC ratio approach. However, the requirement for chest CT measures limits the applicability of the classification in some scenarios.

Objective. To determine whether the diagnostic benefits of MD-COPD over the fixed ratio can be preserved without chest CT, and whether the Emphysema Severity Index (ESI), a spirometric measure derived from the shape of the expiratory flow-volume curve, can substitute for the CT criteria.

Methods. Among 9,240 ever-smokers in COPDGene we compared four classifications of the same participants: the fixed ratio; MD-COPD as published; the NoCT classification, a modified classification with the two CT criteria removed; and the ESI classification, with ESI substituted for the CT criteria. Thresholds for the two “CT-free” classifications were fitted to reproduce the MD-COPD categories. Outcomes were all-cause mortality, respiratory mortality, prospective exacerbations, and FEV₁ decline.

Results. Overall, the ESI classification correctly classified 87.9% of subjects compared to 83.9% for the NoCT classification (p<0.005), and substituting an ESI threshold for CT criteria preserved most of the clinical associations previously reported for MD-COPD categories. Relative to the NoCT classification, most of the improvement of the ESI-based classification was in the classification for subjects with airflow limitation and COPD (COPD major pathway, subgroup F1 score 0.94 vs 0.88, P < 0.001) and subjects with airflow limitation but no minor diagnostic criteria (airflow limitation only, AFL-only, F1 score 0.47 vs 0.40, P < 0.001). The ESI-based classification also closely mirrored the risk profile of the MD-COPD diagnostic pathway groups, but the NoCT classification misclassified an AFL-only subset with markedly increased risk of COPD-related outcomes for all-cause mortality (relative risk 1.57, 95% CI: 1.35 - 1.82), respiratory mortality (relative risk 10.00, 95% CI: 4.95 - 21.87), and respiratory exacerbations (relative risk 1.75, 95% CI: 1.46 - 2.03).

Conclusions. Many of the advantages of a multidimensional framework over the fixed ratio can be preserved with ESI measures replacing chest CT criteria, but not by symptom criteria alone.
