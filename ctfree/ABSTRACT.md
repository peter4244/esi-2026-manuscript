# Abstract — CT-free MD-COPD paper

*(Massimo's v4 (2) of 2026-10-03, his response to Surya Bhatt's critiques, with the fixes Pete approved; v5.)*

**Rationale:** A multidimensional COPD diagnostic framework integrates airflow limitation, symptoms, and chest computed tomography (CT), providing better alignment of COPD diagnosis with disease-related risk than spirometric airflow limitation alone. However, implementation may be challenging when CT is unavailable.

**Objectives:** To determine whether the Emphysema Severity Index (ESI), derived from the expiratory portion of the flow-volume curve, can preserve CT-contributed information when CT is unavailable.

**Methods:** Among 9,240 ever-smokers in COPDGene, we compared the original CT-based framework (MD-COPD-CT) with two modified classifications: one removing CT criteria without replacement (MD-NoCT) and one replacing the CT criteria with ESI (MD-COPD-ESI). Mortality, respiratory exacerbations, and FEV₁ decline were assessed.

**Measurements and Main Results:** Overall agreement with MD-COPD-CT was high and modestly higher with MD-COPD-ESI than MD-NoCT (89% versus 85%, P<0.005). This difference was concentrated among participants with airflow limitation. MD-NoCT reassigned 833 participants meeting MD-COPD-CT criteria for COPD to airflow limitation without additional diagnostic criteria (AFL-only), versus 350 using MD-COPD-ESI. ESI discriminated visual emphysema and airway wall thickening with AUCs of 0.78 and 0.80. Among AFL-only participants, those classified by MD-NoCT had higher unadjusted mortality and exacerbation rates than those classified by MD-COPD-CT (rate ratios 1.38 [95% CI 1.10–1.77] and 1.42 [1.15–1.79]), whereas those classified by MD-COPD-ESI did not (1.14 [0.91–1.44] and 0.96 [0.78–1.21]).

**Conclusions:** When CT is unavailable, ESI recovers part of the information contributed by CT to the multidimensional COPD diagnostic framework, particularly among participants with airflow limitation lacking other diagnostic criteria, without requiring testing beyond conventional spirometry.
