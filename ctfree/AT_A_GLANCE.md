## At a Glance Commentary

Scientific Knowledge on the Subject

Multidimensional approaches to COPD diagnosis integrate airflow limitation, respiratory symptoms, and structural abnormalities on computed tomography (CT). However, CT may not always be available. Whether information contributed by CT can be partly recovered from the same spirometric examination used to assess airflow limitation has not been established.

What This Study Adds to the Field

The Emphysema Severity Index (ESI), derived from the expiratory portion of the spirometric flow-volume curve, recovered part of the information lost when CT criteria were removed from a multidimensional COPD classification. This effect was most evident in participants with airflow limitation, in whom incorporating ESI reduced reassignment to a lower-risk diagnostic category and better preserved the risk profile of that category. CT depicts structural abnormalities, whereas ESI reflects their expression within expiratory physiology. These findings suggest that the expiratory flow-volume curve contains clinically relevant physiological information related to the structural dimension of COPD beyond conventional spirometric indices. When CT is unavailable, this information can be obtained from conventional spirometry without requiring an additional diagnostic procedure.
