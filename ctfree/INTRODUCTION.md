# Introduction — CT-free MD-COPD paper

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a multidimensional disease in which airflow limitation, structural lung abnormalities, and respiratory symptoms provide complementary information on disease expression (1–6). Reliance on spirometry alone does not fully capture COPD heterogeneity, supporting diagnostic approaches that integrate multiple disease dimensions (7–10). Bhatt and colleagues recently proposed a multidimensional diagnostic framework (MD-COPD) integrating spirometry, respiratory symptoms, and chest computed tomography (CT) (11). This framework aligns COPD diagnosis with disease-related risk better than the conventional fixed ratio of forced expiratory volume in 1 second (FEV₁) to forced vital capacity (FVC) and has been independently validated (12). MD-COPD achieves this in part by removing the COPD diagnosis from individuals with airflow limitation but no other diagnostic criteria (airflow limitation only, AFL-only), who in the original report did not have increased all-cause or respiratory mortality (11). However, its requirement for chest CT may limit implementation in clinical practice, epidemiological studies, and settings where imaging is unavailable.

The Emphysema Severity Index (ESI) is a continuous spirometric measure derived from the morphology of the expiratory portion of the flow-volume curve (13, 14). Unlike conventional spirometric indices, ESI is calculated from absolute measurements and is independent of ethnic and anthropometric reference equations. Its associations with CT-defined lung abnormalities (13, 14) and clinically relevant outcomes (15, 16) suggest that ESI may provide a physiological representation of some of the information captured by chest CT.

We hypothesized that ESI could substitute for the CT criteria of MD-COPD when CT is unavailable. In COPDGene, we compared MD-COPD with two modified classifications: one in which CT criteria were removed without replacement (NoCT) and one in which ESI represented the CT component. We compared their ability to reproduce MD-COPD diagnostic pathways, in particular the AFL-only group, and their associations with mortality, respiratory exacerbations, and longitudinal FEV₁ decline.
