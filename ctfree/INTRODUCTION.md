# Introduction — CT-free MD-COPD paper

*(Massimo's v4 (2) of 2026-10-03, his response to Surya Bhatt's critiques, with the fixes Pete approved; v5.)*

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a multidimensional disease in which airflow limitation, structural lung abnormalities, and respiratory symptoms provide complementary information on disease expression (1–6). Reliance on spirometry alone does not fully capture COPD heterogeneity, supporting diagnostic approaches that integrate multiple disease dimensions (7–10). Bhatt and colleagues recently proposed a multidimensional diagnostic framework integrating spirometry, respiratory symptoms, and chest computed tomography (CT), here termed MD-COPD-CT (11). This framework aligns COPD diagnosis with disease-related risk better than spirometric airflow limitation alone and has been independently validated (12, 13). MD-COPD-CT refines COPD classification in both directions, distinguishing individuals with airflow limitation but no other diagnostic criteria as airflow limitation only (AFL-only), while identifying COPD in some individuals without airflow limitation who have multiple additional diagnostic criteria (11). In the original report, AFL-only individuals did not have increased all-cause or respiratory mortality (11). However, implementation of the framework may be challenging when CT is unavailable.

The Emphysema Severity Index (ESI) is a continuous spirometric measure derived from the morphology of the expiratory portion of the flow-volume curve (14, 15). Unlike conventional spirometric indices, ESI is calculated from absolute measurements and is independent of ethnic and anthropometric reference equations. Its associations with CT-defined lung abnormalities (14, 15) and clinically relevant outcomes (16, 17) suggest that ESI may provide a physiological representation of some of the information captured by chest CT.

We hypothesized that incorporating ESI into this multidimensional framework when CT is unavailable could preserve part of the information contributed by the CT criteria through the physiological expression of structural abnormalities in the expiratory flow-volume curve. In COPDGene, we compared MD-COPD-CT with two modified classifications: one in which CT criteria were removed without replacement (MD-NoCT) and one in which CT criteria were replaced by ESI (MD-COPD-ESI). We compared their ability to reproduce MD-COPD-CT diagnostic pathways and their associations with mortality, respiratory exacerbations, and longitudinal FEV₁ decline.
