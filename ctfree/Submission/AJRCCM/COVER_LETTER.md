We are pleased to submit our manuscript, "The Emphysema Severity Index: Representing CT Abnormalities in a Multidimensional COPD Framework," for consideration as an Original Research article in the American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine.

The multidimensional COPD diagnostic framework recently proposed by the COPDGene 2025 Diagnosis Working Group combines airflow limitation, respiratory symptoms, and chest computed tomography (CT) findings, and aligns COPD diagnosis with disease-related risk better than spirometry alone. Its CT criteria limit its use where CT is not available. In 9,240 COPDGene participants, we tested whether the Emphysema Severity Index (ESI), derived from the expiratory flow-volume curve of standard spirometry, can take the place of the CT criteria.

Replacing the CT criteria with ESI reproduced the CT-based classification more closely than removing them (89% versus 85% agreement), and the difference was concentrated in participants with airflow limitation. Removing the CT criteria reassigned 833 participants with CT-based COPD to airflow limitation without other diagnostic criteria, compared with 350 when ESI was used. Among participants with airflow limitation only, those classified without CT had higher mortality and exacerbation rates than those classified with CT, whereas those classified with ESI did not. We believe these findings will interest the Journal's readers because they bear on how a multidimensional COPD diagnosis can be applied where CT is not routinely obtained.
