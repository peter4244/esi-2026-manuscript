We are pleased to submit our manuscript, "The Emphysema Severity Index: Representing CT Abnormalities in a Multidimensional COPD Framework," for consideration as an Original Research article in the American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine.

The multidimensional COPD diagnostic framework recently proposed by the COPDGene 2025 Diagnosis Working Group combines airflow limitation, respiratory symptoms, and chest computed tomography (CT) findings, and aligns COPD diagnosis with disease-related risk better than spirometry alone. However, this framework can only be applied when chest CT data are available, so the purpose of this study was to develop and evaluate a version of this diagnostic classification that can be used when CT is not available. In 9,240 COPDGene participants, we tested whether the Emphysema Severity Index (ESI), which is derived from the expiratory flow-volume curve of standard spirometry, can take the place of the CT criteria.

In brief, we report that, by substituting ESI for the CT criteria, a modified version of these diagnostic criteria closely reproduce the original classification, and we thoroughly analyze the differences by diagnostic strata. We believe these findings will interest the Journal's readers because they bear on how a multidimensional COPD diagnosis can be applied where CT is not routinely obtained.
