## Study population and classification

The analytic cohort included 9,240 participants: 1,119 with preserved ratio impaired spirometry (PRISm), 4,037 with preserved spirometry (formerly referred to as Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease [GOLD] grade 0), 734 with GOLD 1, 1,772 with GOLD 2, 1,050 with GOLD 3, and 528 with GOLD 4. The distribution of participants across diagnostic pathway categories is shown in Table 1. Mean ESI increased across GOLD strata, from 0.90 in GOLD 0 to 8.30 in GOLD 4 (Table S1). ESI changed little with bronchodilation, although the change was statistically significant (mean change −0.09 [95% CI −0.11 to −0.08]). For the modified classifications, the thresholds selected by macro-F1 were ≥2 of 3 symptom criteria for COPD-minor in MD-NoCT and ESI ≥1.50 for the ESI criterion in MD-COPD-ESI (Table S2).

## Relationship of ESI to the visual CT criteria

Mean ESI increased progressively with visually assessed emphysema severity, from 1.04 in participants with no emphysema to 6.62 in those with advanced destructive emphysema; and it also increased across categories of visual airway wall thickening, from 0.96 when airway thickening was absent to 3.32 when definite. ESI increased across increasing severity categories of both visual emphysema and airway wall thickening, although the magnitude of the increase was greater across emphysema severity categories (Figure 1). Using the MD-COPD-CT criteria of emphysema of at least mild severity and definite airway wall thickening, ESI discriminated visual emphysema and wall thickening with AUCs of 0.78 and 0.80 in the overall cohort. When stratifying by the presence of airflow limitation, the discrimination performance for ESI was better in subjects with airflow limitation (AUC = 0.75 for both criteria) than among those without airflow limitation (AUC 0.56 and 0.61, respectively) (Figure 1).

## Agreement between MD-COPD-CT and modified classifications

Cross-classification of the modified classifications with MD-COPD-CT is shown in Figure 2 and Table S3. Overall agreement with MD-COPD-CT was modestly higher with MD-COPD-ESI than with MD-NoCT: 89.0% (8,222 of 9,240) versus 85.0% (7,852 of 9,240), respectively (P < 0.005). For the noCOPD group, sensitivity was 89.3% with MD-NoCT and 88.5% with MD-COPD-ESI and positive predictive value 97.7% and 97.8%, respectively. For COPD-minor group, sensitivity was 88.7% and 89.4% and positive predictive value 60.4% and 58.8%. Differences between the two modified classifications were more pronounced among participants with airflow limitation. For AFL-only, sensitivity was 100% with MD-NoCT and 70.2% with MD-COPD-ESI, whereas positive predictive value was 24.8% and 35.5%, respectively (P < 0.005). For COPD-major, sensitivity was 78.1% with MD-NoCT and 90.8% with MD-COPD-ESI, whereas positive predictive value was 100% and 97.7%, respectively. Both of these results reflect that, in the absence of available CT criteria, the MD-NoCT classification could not identify participants whose only minor criteria were related to structural lung disease, and it accordingly reassigned 833 participants classified as COPD-major by MD-COPD-CT (21.9%) to AFL-only compared with 350 (9.2%) with MD-COPD-ESI (Figure 2 and Table S3).

## Clinical outcomes

Median follow-up was 11.2 years for all-cause mortality, 8.6 years for respiratory mortality, 10.4 years for exacerbations, and 9.7 years for lung function decline. COPD-minor and COPD-major were associated with increased all-cause mortality and exacerbation risk relative to the common noCOPD reference under all three classifications, with higher estimates in COPD-major (Figure 3; Tables S4 and S5; Figure S1).

Differences among classifications were most apparent in AFL-only. Compared with MD-COPD-CT AFL-only, MD-NoCT AFL-only had higher unadjusted all-cause mortality and exacerbation rates (rate ratios 1.38 [1.11–1.80] and 1.42 [1.16–1.82], respectively; both P < 0.005), whereas MD-COPD-ESI AFL-only did not differ significantly from MD-COPD-CT (1.14 [0.92–1.50] and 0.96 [0.79–1.22], respectively; both P > 0.2) (Table S4). After adjustment, MD-NoCT AFL-only continued to have a higher exacerbation risk than MD-COPD-CT AFL-only (ratio of IRRs 1.33 [1.09–1.71]; P = 0.006), whereas the difference in all-cause mortality was no longer significant (ratio of HRs 1.22 [0.98–1.58]; P = 0.074). MD-COPD-ESI AFL-only did not differ significantly from MD-COPD-CT AFL-only for either adjusted outcome (ratio of HRs 1.03 [0.82–1.35]; ratio of IRRs 0.92 [0.75–1.18]; both P > 0.2) (Table S5; Figure S1). Respiratory deaths were too few for statistical comparison; rates were 0.39 per 100 person-years for MD-NoCT AFL-only and 0.09 for both MD-COPD-CT and MD-COPD-ESI AFL-only.

Longitudinal FEV₁ decline differed across diagnostic pathways (Table S6; sensitivity analysis in Table S7). COPD-major showed greater decline than the common noCOPD reference under all three classifications. Greater decline in COPD-minor was observed with MD-COPD-CT but not with MD-NoCT or MD-COPD-ESI, whereas in AFL-only it was observed with MD-NoCT and MD-COPD-ESI but not with MD-COPD-CT. Including participant-specific random slopes gave the same pattern of differences, with larger estimates of excess decline, particularly in COPD-major (Table S7).

## Discordant MD-COPD-CT and MD-COPD-ESI classifications

Clinical outcomes among participants with concordant and discordant MD-COPD-CT and MD-COPD-ESI classifications are shown in Figure 4 and Table S8, stratified by airflow limitation. Among participants without airflow limitation, both discordant groups had increased all-cause mortality and exacerbation rates compared with participants classified as noCOPD by both approaches. Among participants with airflow limitation, neither discordant group had significantly increased all-cause mortality or exacerbation rates compared with participants classified as AFL-only by both approaches, whereas participants classified as COPD by both approaches had increased mortality and exacerbation rates (Figure 4; Table S8).

Clinical, physiological, and CT characteristics of concordant and discordant groups are shown in Figure S2. Among participants without airflow limitation, ESI did not differ significantly between the two discordant groups, whereas visual emphysema and definite airway wall thickening were more frequent in the CT-only group and symptom criteria were more frequent in the ESI-only group. Among participants with airflow limitation, ESI was higher and visual emphysema less frequent in the ESI-only group, whereas %LAA-950HU did not differ significantly between the discordant groups (Figure S2).
