## Study population and classification

The analytic cohort included 9,240 participants: 1,119 with PRISm, 4,037 with GOLD 0, 734 with GOLD 1, 1,772 with GOLD 2, 1,050 with GOLD 3, and 528 with GOLD 4. The distribution of participants across diagnostic categories under the four classifications is shown in Table 1. Mean ESI increased across GOLD strata, from 0.90 in GOLD 0 to 8.30 in GOLD 4 (Table S1). ESI changed little with bronchodilation (mean change, −0.09; SD, 0.82).

The selected thresholds were ≥2 of 3 symptom criteria for NoCT and ESI ≥1.50 for the ESI classification, corresponding to the highest macro-averaged F1 values (Table S2).

## Relationship of ESI to the visual CT criteria

Mean ESI increased progressively with visually assessed emphysema severity, from 1.04 with no emphysema to 6.62 with advanced destructive emphysema, and across categories of visual airway wall thickening, from 0.96 when absent to 3.32 when definite. ESI discriminated visual emphysema and definite airway wall thickening with AUCs of 0.78 and 0.80, respectively, in the overall cohort and 0.75 for both criteria among participants with airflow limitation. Discrimination was lower with preserved spirometry (AUC, 0.56 and 0.61, respectively) (Figure 1).

## Agreement between MD-COPD and CT-free classifications

Cross-classification with MD-COPD is shown in Figure 2 and Table S3. Overall agreement was 89.0% (8,222 of 9,240) for ESI and 85.0% (7,852 of 9,240) for NoCT (P < 0.005). Positive predictive values were >97% for noCOPD and COPD-major with both classifications and were similar for COPD-minor (58.8% with ESI vs 60.4% with NoCT). The largest difference was in AFL-only (35.5% vs 24.8%, respectively; P < 0.005). Both CT-free classifications assigned more participants to AFL-only than the 275 assigned by MD-COPD. Of the 543 AFL-only participants under ESI, 193 were AFL-only and 350 were COPD-major under MD-COPD, compared with 275 and 833 of the 1,108 under NoCT (Figure 2; Table S3). Sensitivity for the MD-COPD AFL-only category was 70.2% with ESI and 100% with NoCT, the latter because the NoCT minor criteria are a subset of those of MD-COPD; for COPD-major the corresponding values were 90.8% and 78.1%. Thus, the main difference between the NoCT and ESI classifications was among participants with airflow limitation: without CT criteria, NoCT moved 833 MD-COPD COPD-major participants into AFL-only, whereas ESI kept 483 of them in COPD-major.

## Clinical outcomes

Median follow-up was 10.8 years for mortality, 10.4 years for exacerbations, and 9.7 years for lung function decline. COPD-minor and COPD-major were associated with increased all-cause mortality and exacerbation risk under all three classifications, with higher estimates in COPD-major (Figure 3; Tables S4 and S5; Figure S1).

Differences among classifications were most apparent in AFL-only. All-cause mortality was 2.29 deaths per 100 person-years in NoCT-defined AFL-only against 1.66 in MD-COPD AFL-only (rate ratio, 1.38; 95% CI, 1.10–1.77; P < 0.005), and exacerbations were 19.5 against 13.7 per 100 person-years (rate ratio, 1.42; 95% CI, 1.15–1.79; P < 0.005). In ESI-defined AFL-only, the corresponding rates of 1.90 and 13.2 did not differ from MD-COPD (rate ratios, 1.14; 95% CI, 0.91–1.44; P = 0.25 and 0.96; 95% CI, 0.78–1.21; P = 0.73) (Table S4). After adjustment, exacerbations remained higher in NoCT-defined than in MD-COPD AFL-only (ratio of IRRs, 1.33; 95% CI, 1.07–1.70; P = 0.008), whereas the difference in all-cause mortality was no longer significant (ratio of HRs, 1.22; 95% CI, 0.97–1.55; P = 0.098); ESI-defined AFL-only again did not differ from MD-COPD (ratio of HRs, 1.03; 95% CI, 0.82–1.31; P = 0.79; ratio of IRRs, 0.92; 95% CI, 0.73–1.17; P = 0.49) (Table S5; Figure S1). Respiratory deaths were too few for comparison with MD-COPD (2 in MD-COPD, 4 in ESI-defined and 34 in NoCT-defined AFL-only). Removing the CT criteria therefore produced an AFL-only group with higher exacerbation rates and, in unadjusted comparisons, higher mortality than that of MD-COPD, whereas substituting ESI produced a more similar AFL-only group whose risk profile was not significantly different from MD-COPD AFL-only.

In COPD-minor and COPD-major, the CT-free groups also differed from the corresponding MD-COPD groups, in opposite directions. All-cause mortality and exacerbation rates in COPD-minor were lower under NoCT (rate ratios, 0.87; 95% CI, 0.79–0.95; P = 0.006 and 0.90; 95% CI, 0.83–0.99; P = 0.036) and ESI (0.88; 95% CI, 0.80–0.97; P = 0.008 and 0.89; 95% CI, 0.81–0.98; P = 0.016) than under MD-COPD (Table S4), but not after adjustment (ratios of HRs and IRRs, 0.92 to 0.95; P = 0.096 to 0.27) (Table S5). Respiratory deaths in COPD-minor were too few for comparison with MD-COPD (8 under MD-COPD, 8 under NoCT, and 9 under ESI). In COPD-major, all-cause mortality, respiratory mortality, and exacerbations were higher than under MD-COPD in both unadjusted and adjusted comparisons, by 17% to 29% under NoCT and by 5% to 9% under ESI (all P < 0.005) (Tables S4 and S5). These higher estimates follow from how the CT-free COPD-major groups were formed: removing CT reassigned 833 MD-COPD COPD-major participants to AFL-only under NoCT and 350 under ESI, and the reassigned participants had lower risk than those who remained (all-cause rate ratio versus the common reference, 1.67 for the 833 reassigned under NoCT against 4.05 for the 2,976 who remained).

Longitudinal FEV₁ decline differed across diagnostic pathways (Table S6). COPD-major showed greater decline than the common noCOPD reference under all three classifications (−5.3, −5.1, and −5.0 mL/yr with MD-COPD, NoCT, and ESI). Greater decline in COPD-minor was observed with MD-COPD (−4.5 mL/yr; P = 0.007) but not with NoCT or ESI (−0.1 mL/yr for both), whereas in AFL-only it was observed with NoCT (−4.9 mL/yr) and ESI (−4.3 mL/yr) but not with MD-COPD (−2.7 mL/yr; P = 0.22).

## Discordant MD-COPD and ESI classifications

Because the ESI classification was the candidate replacement for MD-COPD, we examined the participants on whom the two classifications disagreed. Clinical outcomes among concordant and discordant MD-COPD and ESI classifications are shown in Figure 4 and Table S7, stratified by airflow limitation. With preserved spirometry, 501 participants were ESI-only-COPD, 85 CT-only-COPD, and 714 Both-COPD. Relative to participants classified as noCOPD by both approaches, ESI-only-COPD and CT-only-COPD had increased all-cause mortality and exacerbation rates, as did Both-COPD (Figure 4; Table S7).

With airflow limitation, 350 participants were CT-only-COPD, 82 ESI-only-COPD, and 3,459 Both-COPD. Relative to participants classified as AFL-only by both approaches, neither discordant group had significantly increased all-cause mortality or exacerbation rates, whereas Both-COPD had increased risks of both outcomes (Figure 4; Table S7).

## Characteristics of concordant and discordant classifications

Clinical, physiological, and CT characteristics of concordant and discordant groups are shown in Figure S2. With preserved spirometry, visual emphysema and airway wall thickening were more frequent in CT-only-COPD, whereas mMRC ≥2, SGRQ ≥25, and chronic bronchitis were more frequent in ESI-only-COPD; ESI did not differ significantly between the discordant groups. With airflow limitation, ESI was higher and visual emphysema less frequent in ESI-only-COPD than in CT-only-COPD, whereas %LAA-950HU did not differ significantly (Figure S2).
