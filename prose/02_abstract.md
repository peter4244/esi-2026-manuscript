# ABSTRACT

## Rationale

Spirometry remains central to the diagnosis of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), but airflow obstruction alone does not fully capture the structural and clinical heterogeneity of the disease. A recently proposed multidimensional diagnostic framework integrating spirometry, chest computed tomography (CT)-defined structural abnormalities, and respiratory symptoms improved disease characterization and prognostic assessment.

## Objectives

To determine whether the CT-defined structural component of this multidimensional COPD framework can be represented by the Emphysema Severity Index (ESI), a spirometry-derived measure based on expiratory flow-volume curve morphology.

## Methods

Using the COPDGene cohort, we represented the CT-defined structural component of the original framework with ESI, while preserving airflow obstruction and symptom criteria. Agreement between CT-based and ESI-based classifications and associations with mortality, exacerbations, and longitudinal lung-function decline were evaluated.

## Measurements and Main Results

The ESI-based framework reproduced the CT-based framework with high agreement (91%, κ=0.82), sensitivity 88%, and specificity 94%. Both classifications showed highly comparable associations with all-cause mortality, respiratory mortality, and prospective exacerbations, whereas differences in longitudinal FEV₁ decline were modest with both frameworks. Concordant classification identified participants with the highest clinical risk, whereas discordant groups showed intermediate risk profiles. Secondary analyses showed that ESI provided additional information in smokers with preserved spirometry by identifying individuals at increased risk of subsequent FEV₁ decline.

## Conclusions

An ESI-based spirometric representation of CT-defined structural abnormalities preserved the diagnostic and prognostic information of a multidimensional COPD framework. Information derived from expiratory flow-volume curve morphology may enable broader implementation of multidimensional COPD assessment without requiring CT imaging.
