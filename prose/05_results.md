# RESULTS



## Study population and relationship between ESI and structural lung abnormalities


The analytic cohort comprised 9,463 COPDGene participants with complete baseline data on all diagnostic criteria of the multidimensional framework ({{REF:S2}}). The cohort spanned the full COPDGene spectrum, including 107 never-smokers, 4,073 smokers without airflow obstruction (GOLD 0), 1,144 participants with PRISm, and 4,139 participants with GOLD 1–4 airflow obstruction. Mean baseline ESI increased monotonically with severity across the spectrum, from 0.83 in never-smokers to 8.29 in GOLD 4 participants.

Across the entire cohort, ESI showed significant associations with quantitative CT measures of emphysema, including the percentage of low-attenuation area below −950 Hounsfield units (%LAA−950HU; r=0.77) and parametric response mapping (PRM)-defined emphysema (r=0.80). The strength of these relationships varied according to disease severity, with weak correlations observed in groups with minimal structural abnormalities (GOLD 0, r=0.08) and stronger relationships among participants with established airflow obstruction (GOLD 3, r=0.58).


## Agreement between CT-based and ESI-based multidimensional classifications


The ESI-based framework reproduced the diagnostic structure of the original CT-based multidimensional classification with high agreement ({{FIGURE:1}}).

The ESI-based framework agreed with the CT-based classification in more than nine out of ten participants (91%; κ=0.82), with a sensitivity of 88% and specificity of 94% for reproducing the CT-based framework.

Within the preserved-spirometry subgroup (COPD-minor pathway), analysis of discordant classifications demonstrated distinct clinical and structural profiles ({{FIGURE:2}}). Bhatt-only-COPD participants showed a high prevalence of visually assessed emphysema, frequently with low quantitative emphysema burden. In contrast, ESI-only-COPD participants showed higher ESI values despite not meeting the CT-defined structural criteria of the original framework.

## Prognostic performance of the ESI-based multidimensional framework


The original CT-based and ESI-based multidimensional classifications yielded comparable associations with mortality and prospective exacerbations ({{TABLE:1}}). Across outcomes, adjusted hazard ratios and incidence-rate ratios for COPD-minor and COPD-major categories were highly similar between the two frameworks. Under both frameworks, the AFL-only-noCOPD category was not significantly associated with increased risk across evaluated outcomes, consistent with its classification outside the multidimensional COPD categories.

{{INSERT:table1}}

Longitudinal FEV₁ decline demonstrated less distinct differences across diagnostic categories than mortality and exacerbation outcomes. Under both frameworks, differences in FEV₁ decline for COPD-minor and COPD-major were modest and not statistically significant after adjustment, whereas AFL-only-noCOPD was not associated with accelerated FEV₁ decline ({{REF:S5}}).

Discrimination was nearly identical between frameworks, with comparable C-index values for all-cause and respiratory mortality. Pre-specified sensitivity analyses yielded consistent findings ({{REFS:S3a,S3b,S3c}}), and comparable results between CT-based and ESI-based classifications were observed across cause-specific mortality analyses, including cardiovascular, cancer-related, and other causes of death ({{REFS:S4a,S4b,S4c}}).


## Outcomes of concordant and discordant classifications


Cross-classification analyses showed graded differences in clinical outcomes among participants classified according to agreement or disagreement between the CT-based and ESI-based frameworks ({{TABLE:2}}).

Compared with participants classified as noCOPD by both frameworks, Both-COPD participants showed increased all-cause mortality, respiratory mortality, and exacerbation risk. Discordantly classified groups showed intermediate clinical risk profiles: Bhatt-only-COPD participants showed increased mortality and exacerbation risk, whereas ESI-only-COPD participants showed higher point estimates for respiratory mortality and exacerbations, although estimates were less precise because of the smaller sample size.


## Secondary analyses of continuous ESI


In secondary analyses evaluating ESI as a continuous measure, it provided prognostic information beyond FEV₁/FVC for selected outcomes ({{REFS:S6a,S6b,S6c,S6d}}). Among GOLD 0 smokers, higher baseline ESI was associated with accelerated subsequent lung-function decline, with each 1-unit increase predicting an additional 4.75 mL/year decline in FEV₁ (p = 0.001) independent of baseline FEV₁/FVC. Although GOLD 0 and PRISm participants showed similar baseline ESI values, PRISm participants demonstrated greater longitudinal increases in ESI over follow-up ({{REF:S7}}). ESI was essentially unaffected by acute bronchodilator response, with a mean change between pre- and post-bronchodilator measurements of only −0.09.
